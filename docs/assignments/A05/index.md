## Objective
The objective of this assignment is to create a bracket through detail-design that holds an applied load through a polyester strap that coincides in between 500 lbf < F < 800 lbf. Against a given Rigid T-Beam while using a safety factor of 4. The load path runs through the five different analyses to conduct: a cylindrical pin (A) , a connecting gusset (B), a T-beam block made up of a main beam (C), a flange (D), and a web (E) that bears into the mounting wall. I determined the minimum dimensions for analyzing stiffness. Then used a similar approach to trace the forces to determine the dimensions using a deflection of .005 in for each feature. Below is a list of a more straight forward objective list given directly by the assignment.

- Conduct stress analysis to determine appropriate dimensions for structural features.
- Generate free body diagrams (FBDs) to visualize forces and constraints for each feature.
- Identify and document known and unknown variables, assumptions, and algebraic models for stress calculations.
- Perform stiffness analysis to establish minimum required dimensions based on deflection constraints.
- Compare stress and stiffness analyses to ensure structural integrity and compliance with given constraints.
- Create detailed multiview sketches illustrating dimensions derived from both stress and stiffness analyses.
- Reflect on and document key engineering lessons learned throughout the process.

## Analyze
For my bracket I selected Aluminum 6061-T6 for my material: (Sy = 40,000 psi, E = 10x10^6 psi) over my two other options ASTM A36 Steel and Ti-6Al-4V. I decided on this material because it carries the given load with a safety factor of 4 without oversizing the part or making it too heavy. I then decided on using F = 650 lbf as my applied force, since it is the midpoint of the given range.
